# GATE CS Historical Research Method

## 1. Distinguish four separate claims
- GATE examination history (when GATE as an examination began).
- Availability of a CS paper in a particular year.
- Availability of a particular session/set PDF.
- Availability of an official answer key for that exact session/set.

Evidence for one claim does not automatically prove the others.

## 2. Search order
1. Official GATE website for the relevant organizing institute/year.
2. Official IISc/IIT historical archive.
3. Official answer-key/response archive.
4. Institutional repositories.
5. Trusted third-party archives as leads.
6. Community discussions for solution review only.

## 3. Per-document record
Record URL, source organization, title, year, paper code, session/set, retrieval timestamp, filename, SHA-256, PDF page count, file size, and inspection notes.

## 4. Per-paper audit
- Confirm cover/header identifies the exam and CS paper.
- Confirm year and session from the document itself.
- Count question numbers and identify optional/sectional structure, if present.
- Record whether diagrams, tables, or formula images are present.
- Record official key version and any final-key revisions.
- Compare the paper with other copies; do not assume two differently hosted PDFs are identical.

## 5. Extraction audit
- Preserve question text verbatim.
- Keep page number and visible question number.
- Keep options as separate records.
- Keep original mathematical/code formatting.
- Store diagram crops with source-page coordinates.
- Compare extracted text visually to each original PDF page.
- Mark OCR uncertainty rather than silently correcting it.

## 6. Answer audit
- Official key is a separate evidence object.
- Record key version (provisional/final/revised) when available.
- Store official answer exactly as published.
- Independently solve separately.
- Flag disputed/ambiguous items; do not silently replace the official answer.

## 7. Copyright and access
Prefer official links and metadata. Do not bypass access controls. Store source URLs and permitted local working copies. Before publishing full question text or PDFs publicly, review applicable copyright and source terms; public availability does not itself grant republication rights.
